# Helpdesk Triage walkthrough, voice script (example)

Audience: customer success and support leads (semi-technical). Target: about 2 minutes.
Voice: your ElevenLabs clone or a stock voice, eleven_multilingual_v2. Budget: 15 characters per target second.

Read as spoken. No bullet voice, no "in this video". Short sentences. Product names said plainly.
The VO generator parses the `## NN Title (…)` headings; keep that shape. Section 01 narrates the title card.

---

## 01 Cold open (title card, 0:00 to 0:12)

When a support ticket comes in, someone has to read it, decide how urgent it is, and send it to the right team. This tool does that first pass for you. It reads every new ticket, sorts it, and hands your team a queue that is already in order.

## 02 Inbox (inbox page, 0:12 to 0:35)

This is the inbox. Every new ticket lands here with a priority, a suggested team, and a one-line summary. You can open any ticket to see why it was sorted the way it was. If you disagree, change the priority or the team, and the tool learns from that change.

## 03 Ticket detail (ticket page, 0:35 to 1:00)

Open a ticket and you see the original message, the summary, and the fields the tool filled in: product, customer plan, and whether the customer has written in before about the same thing. The reply box at the bottom starts with a suggested first response that you can edit or replace.

## 04 Team queues (queues page, 1:00 to 1:25)

Each team has its own queue, ordered by priority and age. A ticket that has waited too long moves up on its own. The numbers at the top show how many tickets are waiting, how many are overdue, and the average time to first reply this week.

## 05 Settings (settings page, 1:25 to 1:50)

Settings is where you tell the tool about your teams and your products. Add a team, name the products it owns, and set how long a ticket can wait before it is marked overdue. Every change is saved with a date and the name of the person who made it, and you can put any earlier version back.

## 06 Close (reports page, 1:50 to 2:05)

The reports page shows the week at a glance. Try it on one team's inbox first. That is the fastest way to see what it changes.

---

Notes for the edit:
- Scene boundaries are soft. If a clip runs long, let the VO breathe over it rather than cutting the clip.
- Invented data only on camera; label role-play rows so they read as such.
- Captures run headless, so no URL bar and no hostnames appear.
